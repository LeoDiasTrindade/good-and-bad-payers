We estimate the probability that a borrower will be 90 days or more past due
within two years, using the 150{,}000 labelled clients of the Kaggle
\emph{Give Me Some Credit} dataset, of whom 6.7\% defaulted. An exploratory
analysis found impossible values, special codes and missing values that carry
information; they were encoded as rule-based flags, and every step that learns
from data was fitted inside cross-validated pipelines to avoid leakage. We
compare a regularized logistic regression, chosen for its odds-ratio
interpretation, with a random forest. Hyperparameters and the decision
threshold were selected by stratified 5-fold cross-validation, and a 20\% test
set was used once. On the test set the logistic regression reaches an AUC of
0.858 and the random forest 0.866, against 0.5 for a constant baseline; at a
threshold that catches 70\% of defaulters, the forest raises 14\% fewer false
alarms. Previous late payments, revolving utilization and age drive both
models. Regularization brought no gain with this many rows per coefficient,
and most of the forest's advantage appears to come from interactions between
features.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We estimated the probability of a serious credit delinquency within two years
for the 150{,}000 labelled clients of the \emph{Give Me Some Credit} dataset.
A careful exploratory analysis turned impossible values, special codes and
informative missing values into explicit rules and flags, and every learned
step was fitted inside cross-validation, with the test set opened once. On
that test set a regularized logistic regression reached an AUC of 0.858 and a
random forest 0.866. The explainable model gives up little ranking power,
previous late payments and revolving utilization dominate both models, and
regularization was unnecessary with this many rows per coefficient. The next
steps are gradient-boosted trees, a threshold derived from the costs of each
error, a check of probability calibration, and an explicit discussion of
whether age should enter a credit decision.
